# Phase 1F.2 — Intelligence Construct Definition

## Frozen working definition

For this benchmark, **intelligence** is a system's externally observable capacity to acquire new competence efficiently, adapt behavior to meaningful task changes, generalize learned structure to unseen instances and domains, and retain or efficiently reacquire that competence under fixed exposure/resource budgets.

This is a **functional, benchmark-bounded construct**, not a claim about consciousness, human equivalence, or a universal intelligence essence. It is defined before analysis by independent task behavior. It is not defined by IG, IGR, IE, CGR, C_t, a formula's growth, or the presence of internal capability objects.

## Necessary behavioral dimensions

1. **Learning:** improve on future tasks after a fixed sequence of informative examples; performance is measured on disjoint probes, not the demonstration items.
2. **Adaptation:** recover competence after a preregistered rule, distribution, task-family, or vocabulary shift, within a fixed evidence budget.
3. **Generalization and transfer:** succeed on held-out instances/templates and disjoint families/domains without target-test exposure.
4. **Future competence acquisition:** acquire an operation, skill, or task family not already mastered at time t, and demonstrate it on held-out tasks.
5. **Persistence and efficiency:** retain or efficiently relearn behavior after a no-refresh delay, under fixed exposure and compute budgets.

The six-category profile in `intelligence_taxonomy.md` is reported component by component. No single scalar is treated as intelligence by definition.

## Behavioral evidence rule

A dimension is supported only by evaluator-scored future task outputs with a frozen rubric, explicit denominator, cluster-aware uncertainty, fixed exposure/resource budget, and comparison to a no-learning/chance or pre-learning baseline. The target score must be computable with the IG implementation removed or disabled.

## Failure criteria

The intelligence construct is unsupported if only IG/IGR rises; if performance is limited to demonstration items or exact repeats; if improvements disappear on held-out variants or after delay; if adaptation/generalization are not tested; if success is explained by prior performance, experience, existing capability, memory, or transfer measures; if the outcome scorer reads IG/IGR/IE/CGR/C_t; or if differential exposure/budget, leakage, or missingness invalidates comparison. Internal metric growth cannot rescue a failed behavioral criterion.
