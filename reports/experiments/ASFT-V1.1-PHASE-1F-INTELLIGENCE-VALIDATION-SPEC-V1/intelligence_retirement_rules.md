# Phase 1F.11 — Intelligence-Claim Retirement Rules

## Purpose and scope

This section commits in advance to what will happen if IG fails independent validation. It evaluates the **claim that IG is a measure/predictor of intelligence growth in the preregistered population and tasks**, not whether systems possess intelligence in every possible sense.

## Claim survives

Retain a bounded intelligence-measure claim only if all are satisfied:

1. IG_t improves prediction of the primary external Future Learning Rate AULC over the full M0 baseline by more than the frozen SOI, with positive-direction uncertainty bounds.
2. The primary result replicates on disjoint task templates/seeds/domains.
3. At least one additional independent adaptation, transfer, or generalization endpoint is a replicated win.
4. Target scoring is demonstrably independent of IG/IGR/IE/CGR/C_t and integrity gates pass.
5. Predictive calibration is not driven solely by one task family, seed, or internal score component.

The surviving claim remains bounded to tested tasks/population; prediction does not establish causality or human-like general intelligence.

## Claim weakens

Weaken, qualify, and do not claim validated intelligence if IG wins only in development or one cohort; only secondary outcomes win; outcomes are inconsistent across domains; IG predicts only formula-derived/internal targets; positive association does not improve held-out performance beyond M0; direction is unstable; metric drift/initialization dominates; or replication is inconclusive.

## Claim retired / metric renamed

IG_t is retired as an intelligence measure if, in **both adequately powered confirmatory and independent replication cohorts**, all six external outcome families A–F show either an equivalent tie (CI wholly inside the SOI region) or a loss against M0, using the frozen `0.02` normalized-outcome SOI or a development-frozen scale-specific SOI, and no independently preregistered external endpoint replicates a win, then:

- retire “IG measures intelligence” and “IG measures intelligence growth” for the tested scope;
- rename the formula output **Internal Growth Composite (IGC)** or another non-construct label;
- stop presenting IG as intelligence or validated learning capacity; IGR/IE also remain non-intelligence bookkeeping measures unless they independently meet their own frozen external survival criteria (IGR cannot rescue the retired IG claim);
- retain the arithmetic only as an internal bookkeeping/diagnostic metric, clearly labeled as such;
- update ASFT claims, diagrams, preregistrations, and downstream interpretations accordingly.

IGR_t is also evaluated as a key-secondary predictor with multiplicity correction. If it meets the same replicated equivalence/loss criterion, retire the “intelligence-growth-rate” label for IGR as well. If IGR alone predicts an external endpoint, it may support a separately named, bounded predictive growth-rate index; it does not rescue the failed IG_t intelligence-measure claim or justify calling either metric intelligence without its own survival criteria.

An underpowered null, missing battery, or integrity failure is **not** evidence for retirement; it is inconclusive/unavailable and blocks the intelligence claim until validly tested. Conversely, a primary win that fails replication weakens the claim but does not meet survival criteria.

## Explicit freedom to reject

> The benchmark is designed to allow IG to be rejected as an intelligence measure if it provides no predictive value beyond capability, experience, memory, transfer, or prior performance. If that happens under the frozen replication rule, the intelligence-growth claim fails and the metric is renamed; the benchmark itself has done its job.
