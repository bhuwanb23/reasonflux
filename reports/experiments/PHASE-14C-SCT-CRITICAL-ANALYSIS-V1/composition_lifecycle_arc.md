# Composition, lifecycle, and ARC — SCT interpretation

## Composition

Phase 13C evaluates only ordered rotation→reflection because counting/grouping skills did not emerge. On 100 paired tasks, composition succeeds on 100%, the best single component on 0%, and the no-skill composition-capable solver on 100%; success-gated normalized step reduction is 0.429. All 10 separate composition-reuse tasks succeed.

Thus the demonstrated result is an ordered pair that runs and reduces measured steps in this simulator. Since the no-skill solver already succeeds on every paired task, composition's success rate does not show it was necessary to solve the tasks. One pair cannot establish general composition or arbitrary multi-skill chains. Phase 13F's no-composition arm loses on its frozen task-utility/composition-task-utility outcomes, adding synthetic support for a composition benefit without broadening the available family pairs.

## Lifecycle

The Phase 13C lifecycle probe marked dormancy in 44/44 cases; 22/44 reactivation attempts succeeded and 22/44 failed. This supports that dormancy/reactivation state machinery was exercised, with mixed reactivation outcomes. It does not demonstrate lifecycle-policy benefit: the 13C no-lifecycle ablation ties primary mean net value at 0.345, and the 13F no-lifecycle arm matches ASFT on recorded outcomes. No robust retirement benefit is shown.

## ARC

Phase 13G's bounded ARC adapter accepted/promoted zero skills and zero capabilities. Exact demonstration fits were 3/600 discovery, 5/200 transfer, and 2/200 holdout, under a compact DSL and frozen promotion conditions; bounded holdout exact-task accuracy was 0.005. Therefore ARC skill formation was not demonstrated in this adapter run. These results are not a general disproof of skill formation on ARC: operation coverage and exact-fit support were very low. Preserve the same-holdout rerun/serialization-failure caveat.
