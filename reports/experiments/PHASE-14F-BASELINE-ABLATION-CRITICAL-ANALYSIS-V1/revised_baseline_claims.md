# Revised Baseline Claims

## Revised claim

> ASFT shows non-random, comparator-specific synthetic learning and transfer behavior. It sometimes exceeds no-learning and random controls, and some invariant-removal contrasts reduce selected transfer or growth metrics. However, Phase 13C raw memory-only retrieval is substantially stronger than SCT on its net-value protocol; compression remains competitive in corrected Phase 13D, while 13E/13F favor ASFT under different endpoints. Several SCT/SCV/lifecycle ablations tie because candidate selection or lifecycle actions do not diverge in the tested pools. Current evidence does not establish universal ASFT superiority, exclusive explanation by its named mechanisms, or causal necessity of several components.

## Explicit boundaries

- “Memory-only” in Phase 13C is raw trace-exemplar retrieval; it is not the Phase 13E/F skill-storage baseline.
- “Random” controls differ by phase and should be named exactly.
- B4 is a compression proxy, not canonical DreamCoder.
- Phase 13B pre-correction and corrected TU magnitudes remain distinct; both tie informed policies and fail the strict gate.
- Phase 13D conclusions use corrected saved-record posthoc comparisons; preserve the frozen dashboard alongside them.
- Positive CGR/IG is not automatically task-performance gain; ARC's inactive pipeline is unavailable evidence, not a baseline win.
