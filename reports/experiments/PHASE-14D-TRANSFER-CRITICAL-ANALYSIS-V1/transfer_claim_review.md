# Transfer Claim Review

## Claim being assessed

ASFT discovers skills from traces, reuses them on related tasks, reduces reasoning cost, detects harmful reuse, and turns transfer into capability growth—potentially extending to ARC.

## Evidence-weighted assessment

1. **Reuse is real within the synthetic setup.** Phase 13C saved 201 correct positive-credit reuses from 223 attempts. Phase 13D corrected results show positive TS where reflection/rotation source skills are present. These observations support reuse under the tested task generator, not universal transfer theory. Positive TS is not necessarily incremental ASFT gain: covered Phase 13D cells tie `BEST_SINGLE`, and some tie or trail B4.
2. **Cross-family evidence is bounded.** Phase 13C's ledger has 91/91 successful attempted cross-family SCT applications (and 308/308 for MEMORY_ONLY). The skill pool is reflection/rotation, the applications are nested within a shared synthetic protocol, and attempt-level credit does not establish broad abstract reasoning.
3. **Cost effect survives the corrected accounting.** 13C and corrected 13D both show positive success-gated reasoning-step-cost proxies. Search effort and external compute savings are not measured.
4. **Coverage matters.** 13D's near/moderate positive cells are eligible only where source skills exist. EXP-TR-07–11 have no active composition; raw TS there cannot be called structural transfer.
5. **Growth link is unresolved.** 13D has positive descriptive TS–CGR correlation but negative mean CGR; 13E's TS–CGR estimate is negative with an interval spanning zero. No causal claim follows.
6. **Comparator wins are not universal.** 13D loses the all-baseline gate on B4; Phase 13F and 13C show endpoint and comparator dependence.
7. **Negative-transfer result is targeted.** Forced-case probability decrements and guard rejections are observed, but exact accuracy NTR is zero and there is no broad estimate of discrimination specificity.
8. **External transfer is not demonstrated.** 13G produced no ARC skills/capabilities; the task pipeline was too sparse to evaluate skill transfer.

## Decision

Retain the narrow claim that generated reflection/rotation skills are reused across related synthetic tasks and can lower a success-gated reasoning-step proxy. Reject stronger claims of growth causality, general helpful/harmful discrimination, structural or multi-skill transfer, universal baseline superiority, or ARC transfer.
