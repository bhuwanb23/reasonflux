## Historical evaluation audit

The read-only Phase 14A–14J audit found that the strongest conclusions are bounded operational facts and limits on inference: 13B repeatedly recovered known-generator operation signatures but failed ranking superiority; 13C formed/reused reflection and rotation skill objects but selected all `44/44` candidates, so SCT/always/random choices collapsed; 13D measured covered synthetic reuse/step-cost proxies but did not establish transfer-caused growth; 13E computed positive internal metrics but did not independently validate capability or intelligence constructs; 13F's no-SCV/random-SCV selection contrast tied in a four-of-four pool; and 13G's bounded ARC adapter had very low fit coverage, a config/code search-budget discrepancy, no downstream ASFT skill chain, and a reused holdout after predictions were generated in memory.

Phase 14 preserves both favorable and adverse evidence, but leaves ambiguity around construct validity, independent units, posthoc corrections, different denominators, statistical precision, and external validity. The full file-by-file evaluation audit is `evaluation_audit.md`.

## Frozen framework delivered

This package defines E1 exploratory, E2 development, E3 confirmatory, and E4 replication; only E3/E4 can support primary claims, and E1/E2 cannot be promoted after outcome access. The preregistration standard freezes the research question, hypothesis, one primary endpoint, secondary/diagnostic outcomes, comparators, independent unit/N rationale, SOI, missingness, decision rules, splits, budgets, data access, analysis, replication, and hashes. The endpoint and statistical frameworks distinguish decision-driving outcomes, nested rows vs independent units, eligible vs attempted denominators, missing vs valid zero, exact ties, zero variance, and clustered uncertainty.

Replication R1–R4 covers within-generator, cross-generator, cross-domain, and external-benchmark verification. Generalization needs relevant R2/R3/R4 evidence; one positive result is not a generalized finding. Interpretation, reporting, and governance rules prohibit metric-to-theory leaps, selective reporting, post-outcome threshold/endpoint/split changes, gate relabeling, and silent corrections. The five-state outcome model adds `non_informative` for degenerate contrasts such as identical decisions.

## Contract results

**67/67 specification contracts pass** across experiment classification, preregistration completeness, endpoint separation, independent-unit/denominator reporting, missingness, five-state outcome logic, replication, governance, and reporting. The constructed matrix can return all five states, a precise equivalence tie, and an alternative-comparator win under its preregistered negative-direction claim. Those values are hand-constructed code-path inputs; no task outcomes, effect estimates, or scientific findings were generated.

## What Phase 1I demonstrates

- An evaluation framework and preregistration/reporting templates exist.
- E1/E2 findings cannot be promoted to primary evidence.
- The five outcome states are executable in the decision contract.
- Negative outcomes can remain reported; unavailable is not failure or zero.
- Replication classes and generalization boundaries are frozen.
- Governance rules prohibit post-outcome reinterpretation and require source lineage.

## What Phase 1I does not demonstrate

It does not demonstrate that IDT or SCT works, memory loses, capability is valid, IG measures intelligence, ARC generalization succeeds, or any Phase 14 scientific hypothesis is true. It does not implement task runners, seal future outcome data, calculate sample sizes for future experiments, or generate empirical replication.
