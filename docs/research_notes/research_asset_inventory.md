## Section B — Interesting observations, not yet claims

- **Boundary toy signal:** the S2 fixture moved false activations 2/4→0/4 with 2/2 applicable contexts still covered. This is suggestive only; four negative cases and one fixed fixture cannot establish generalization or novelty.
- **Ranking behavior depends on candidate-pool pressure:** transfer-aware and frequency-only ranking tied when eligible candidates led to identical choices. The injected frequent-no-op stress test shows sensitivity to a constructed distractor, not superiority on natural or external candidate populations.
- **Candidate availability is an upstream bottleneck:** the audited ARC invariant path yielded zero accepted candidates under the tested settings. Generic patterns and relaxed proposals were not valid replacements for the registered invariant object. This says the current pipeline could not supply the intended benchmark population; it does not say the representation is impossible.
- **Synthetic positive-control yield is not production yield:** ASFT v1.1 Phase 2I-A's HCC control produced matching candidates in both arms using oracle-selected one-action fixtures. It tests plumbing, not R1/R3 search, end-to-end trace integration, ARC candidate yield, or scientific benefit.
- **Capability metrics vary across cohorts:** positive internal `C_t`/CGR behavior in one cohort coexists with negative mean CGR in corrected Phase 13D and a persistent capability object. Metric values should not be equated with robust capability growth.
- **Memory remains a live alternative:** memory-only performed strongly on the Phase 13C endpoint, but opportunity counts and later memory implementations differ. The record supports careful comparison, not a universal ranking of memory above ASFT.
- **The ARC failure is underdetermined:** very low full-DSL demonstration-fit coverage, a discovery budget mismatch, reused holdout exposure, and no downstream candidates mean ARC did not instantiate the intended mechanism chain. This is a concrete validity/engineering diagnosis, not evidence that a particular representation alone caused failure.
- **The S3 null is unusually interpretable but narrow:** an independent, frozen, fair comparison produced the same score for invariant and replay arms on every one of 105 cases. The unexplained generalization to other tasks/representations is not a surviving Claim N result and cannot be recovered by post-hoc subsets or an N-v2 rescue.

## Section C — Reusable infrastructure

- **Synthetic trace-to-skill research prototype:** event/trace logging, operation-pattern mining, reflection/rotation skill construction, reuse accounting, and simulator cost-proxy measurement. Phase 11 reports 24 modules implemented, six MVP gates demonstrated, and 501 tests passing; those are implementation facts, not validation of the theory.
- **Comparators and baseline runners:** replay/memory, no-learning, random skill creation, skill storage, and DreamCoder-like baseline scaffolds, plus saved ablation and failure-analysis packages. Their fairness and opportunity structures remain protocol-specific and should not be conflated across phases.
- **Claim/evidence and publication-ceiling framework:** claim maps, endpoint definitions, preregistration/readiness checklists, validity/threat registers, safe-conclusion tables, and explicit separation of implementation evidence from scientific evidence.
- **Execution integrity and provenance:** manifests, source hashes, frozen configs, logs, prediction-before-score records, per-cell reports, and fail-closed execution protocols. S3 preserves one authorized run with no deviations or reruns; its permanent artifacts remain in `reports/experiments/T2T-S3-EXEC-2026-11-05-001/`.
- **ASFT v1.1 representation/runner plumbing:** R1/R3 adapters, parser and bounded-search contracts, common semantic-trace validation, train-only source handling, resource limits, synthetic HCC control, and artifact/hash auditing. Phase 2I-A reports 68 synthetic regression tests passing and 52/52 provenance hashes matching; it has not performed the ARC run it was built to support.
- **ARC audit assets:** task/source manifests, coverage accounting, candidate-yield audits, search-budget reviews, and representation/validity analyses. These make the failed or blocked external test diagnosable; they do not themselves constitute ARC evidence for ASFT.
- **Durable research record:** phase-by-phase artifacts and final synthesis in `reports/experiments/PHASE-14J-FINAL-SCIENTIFIC-CONCLUSIONS-V1/`, T2T claim/evidence files under `docs/05_trace_to_transfer/`, and BL1's four P0 artifacts under `docs/05_trace_to_transfer/bl1/`.

## Section D — Open questions

**No open scientific question currently meets all three inclusion tests: it survived testing, is not obviously covered by prior art, and has a plausible paper-scale contribution.** The remaining gaps are real but do not qualify as surviving questions under this filter:

- The ASFT transfer→capability and capability→intelligence links were not established; internal metric behavior lacks independent construct validation. These are unvalidated links, not phenomena that survived a decisive test.
- ARC invariant-candidate production and the R1/R3 comparison remain blocked before the scientific comparison. The present record is a pipeline/readiness bottleneck, not a tested representation result.
- T2T's Claim N is closed as N-FAIL for its finite synthetic estimand; broader transfer was outside that estimand and cannot be treated as an open positive claim.
- BL1's proposed boundary-learning contribution is substantially covered by prior art; the toy signal is not a surviving decision-grade effect.

**Answer to the success criterion:** after the work recorded here, there is no sufficiently evidenced, demonstrably uncovered phenomenon that can honestly be called “what remains unexplained” and advanced as the next paper program. There are unresolved limitations and untested generalizations, but no qualifying open question is identified by this inventory.
